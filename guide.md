# Custom Application Development Firm: Technical Interview Questions to Ask

![Custom Application Development Firm: Technical Interview Questions to Ask](./banner.png)

## Introduction

Choosing a development partner is one of the most expensive decisions an early-stage company makes. The wrong firm doesn't just deliver late. It ships a fragile codebase, burns your runway, and leaves technical debt that takes years to unwind.

Most founders vet agencies on portfolios and price. Portfolios show polish, not engineering discipline, and the cheapest quote often hides the costliest rebuild.

If you are evaluating [**custom mobile app development services**](https://mtechub.com/services/mobile-app-development/), the technical interview is your best risk-control tool. These 10 questions show how a firm actually builds, communicates, and handles things going wrong.

---

## TL;DR: Executive Summary

Ask these 10 questions in every agency interview:

1. **Process and communication:** How do you go from idea to launch, and how will I see progress?
2. **Architecture and scalability:** How do you choose the stack, and what happens at 10x growth?
3. **Ownership:** Who owns the code, IP, and infrastructure accounts?
4. **Team:** Who exactly will work on my project?
5. **Quality:** How do you test, and what does "done" mean?
6. **Security:** How do you protect user data and meet compliance requirements?
7. **Scope and budget:** How do you estimate, and what happens when scope changes?
8. **Store deployment:** Who handles App Store and Google Play submission?
9. **Post-launch:** What support, warranty, and handover do you provide?
10. **Proof:** Can you show live apps and put me in touch with past clients?

**Rule of thumb:** A strong firm answers with specifics, examples, and trade-offs. A weak firm answers with buzzwords, guarantees, and deflection.

## The 10 Critical Questions

### 1. "Walk me through your process from discovery to launch, and how will we track progress?"

**What to ask**
Ask for each phase: discovery, design, development, QA, release, and iteration. Ask for real artifacts from a past project, such as user stories, wireframes, and sprint reports.

Then ask how you'll see progress: demo frequency, project board access (Jira, Linear, Trello), staging builds, and communication channels.

**Why it matters**
Process is the difference between predictable delivery and chaos. Without discovery, requirements drift and budgets balloon. Without visibility, you find problems when they are most expensive to fix.

**Red flags**
- No discovery or requirements phase ("we'll just start building")
- No sample documentation, sprint reports, or roadmap from past projects
- "We're agile" with no cadence, ceremonies, or deliverables
- Monthly reports with no access to working builds
- A single point of contact who filters all information

---

### 2. "How do you choose the tech stack, and how will the architecture scale when we grow 10x?"

**What to ask**
Ask which frameworks, languages, and cloud services they recommend and which alternatives they rejected. Ask about native (Swift, Kotlin) versus cross-platform (Flutter, React Native) trade-offs for your use case.

Then ask about scaling: database strategy, caching, API design, and load testing. Request a case study of a product that outgrew its original expectations.

**Why it matters**
The stack drives hiring costs, performance, maintainability, and vendor lock-in. An architecture that can't scale forces an expensive rewrite at the moment you need momentum.

**Red flags**
- The same stack recommended for every client
- No ability to explain trade-offs or name downsides of their own choice
- Proprietary frameworks or low-code tools that make migration difficult
- "We'll deal with scaling later"
- Microservices for an unvalidated MVP, or no load testing experience

---

### 3. "Who owns the source code, IP, and infrastructure accounts?"

**What to ask**
Ask when full IP ownership transfers to you and whether the contract states it. Confirm that app store accounts, cloud environments, repositories, and third-party licenses are registered under **your** company.

**Why it matters**
Ownership disputes can freeze your product. If the agency controls your repositories or developer accounts, switching vendors becomes a hostage negotiation.

**Red flags**
- IP transfers only after "final payment" with vague terms
- Apps published under the agency's developer account
- Reluctance to grant admin access to repos and cloud services
- Reliance on proprietary "internal frameworks" with no license granted to you

---

### 4. "Who exactly will work on my project?"

**What to ask**
Request names, roles, seniority, and time allocation for the engineers, designers, QA, and project manager assigned to you. Ask whether any work is subcontracted or offshored.

**Why it matters**
Agencies often sell with senior talent and staff with juniors. Team turnover mid-project means lost context, lower code quality, and delays.

**Red flags**
- "We'll assign the team after signing"
- Senior engineers in the pitch but not in the delivery plan
- Undisclosed subcontracting
- No plan for what happens if a key developer leaves

---

### 5. "How do you test, and what is your definition of 'done'?"

**What to ask**
Ask about unit tests, integration tests, automated UI testing, device coverage, code review, and CI/CD pipelines. Ask how bugs are tracked and prioritized.

**Why it matters**
Untested code ships straight to your users. Bugs found after launch cost far more than bugs caught in review, and crashes and poor store reviews hurt early growth.

**Red flags**
- Developers test their own code with no dedicated QA
- No automated testing or CI/CD pipeline
- No code review policy
- "Done" means "it works on my phone"

---

### 6. "How do you handle security, data privacy, and compliance?"

**What to ask**
Ask about authentication, encryption in transit and at rest, secrets management, dependency scanning, and secure API design. Ask about OWASP Mobile guidelines and, where relevant, GDPR, HIPAA, SOC 2, or PCI-DSS experience.

**Why it matters**
A breach can end a startup through fines, lost trust, and legal exposure. Compliance is far cheaper to design in from day one than to retrofit.

**Red flags**
- Security described as "something we add at the end"
- No familiarity with regulations in your industry
- No mention of penetration testing, dependency audits, or threat modeling
- Credentials or API keys stored in code repositories
- Compliance left entirely to your team

---

### 7. "How do you estimate timelines and budgets, and what happens when scope changes?"

**What to ask**
Ask how estimates are built and whether they use fixed-price, time-and-materials, or hybrid models. Ask how change requests are documented and priced, and about a recent project that went over budget and why.

**Why it matters**
Budget overruns are the most common source of agency-client conflict. A clear change-control process protects both sides and prevents surprise invoices.

**Red flags**
- A fixed quote after a 20-minute call
- Prices far below competitors with no explanation
- Time-and-materials billing with no milestones or estimates
- No formal change-request process
- Claims of never missing a deadline or going over budget

---

### 8. "Who handles App Store and Google Play deployment?"

**What to ask**
Ask who manages store submissions, provisioning profiles, signing certificates, metadata, and screenshots. Ask how they handle review rejections.

**Why it matters**
Apple and Google enforce strict guidelines, and a rejection can delay launch by days or weeks. Experience with the review process is a practical, often overlooked skill.

**Red flags**
- The final build is handed over and store submission is left to you
- No experience with rejections or guideline changes
- Signing keys and certificates held by the agency
- No plan for phased rollouts or beta testing (TestFlight, Play Console tracks)

---

### 9. "What happens after launch? What support and handover do you provide?"

**What to ask**
Ask about warranty periods, bug-fix policy, maintenance plans, and SLAs. Confirm you will receive architecture docs, API documentation, and deployment runbooks.

**Why it matters**
Launch is the beginning, not the end. Apps need OS compatibility updates, SDK changes, security patches, and iteration based on user feedback. Poor handover creates dependency on one vendor.

**Red flags**
- Support ends the day the app is submitted
- No bug-fix warranty (30 to 90 days is a common benchmark)
- Documentation and runbooks are an optional paid extra
- No clear SLA for critical bugs or outages

---

### 10. "Can you show live apps and connect me with past clients?"

**What to ask**
Ask for links to live apps in the stores, detailed case studies, and two or three client references you can call directly. Ask those clients about communication, deadlines, and how the agency handled problems.

**Why it matters**
Past performance is the most reliable predictor of future results. References reveal what portfolios never show: how the agency behaves under pressure.

**Red flags**
- Only static mockups, no live apps
- References declined or replaced with written testimonials only
- Vague "confidentiality" excuses for every past project
- Case studies with no measurable outcomes

## How to Score Your Interviews

Rate each agency from 1 to 5 on every question, then compare. Weight the questions by your stage. Ownership, security, and team transparency are usually non-negotiable.

Pay attention to *how* they answer. Strong agencies ask you questions back, challenge weak assumptions, and admit trade-offs. A firm that agrees with everything you say is a warning sign.
